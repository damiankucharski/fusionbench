# Does reusing scenes for checkpoint and fusion selection hurt fresh-scene performance?

Frozen before running the study. This is a manual round. The periodic research switch remains off.

## Paired design

Use 32 independent banks, integer seeds 284100 to 284131 inclusive. Each bank has three independent same-law blocks. A and B each have 24 scenes of eight correlated binary observations. I has 256 new scenes of eight. Eight providers each release eight synthetic checkpoint variants with equal marginal competence. The first released checkpoint gives the nested one-choice control. In each selectable block pick the best of the first K slots per provider by pooled positive-class F1 (positive when p > 0.5, earliest slot wins ties). K is 1 or 8. These are draws of predictions, not saved states of trained networks. Details of the joint law and random streams are in the generator.

For each K, pick checkpoints on A, freeze those exact columns across all blocks, and fit fusion either on A (reuse) or B (disjoint). Repeat picking on B and fitting on B or A. All fusion methods in a cell have identical arrays. Both stages use 192 rows; reuse consumes 24 distinct development scenes, disjoint consumes 48. I supplies no optimization, model configuration, thresholds or early stopping. Pairing fixes fusion/search and inner split seeds across the compared checkpoint arms for a given bank (seed = bank seed + 1000000). Fold swaps are dependent and get averaged before inference. K=1 has exactly zero mean-of-swapped-arms contrast bank by bank by construction, even if the two fit-block scores differ. There is one search seed per bank, not an independent sweep of search seeds.

Write z = 0.9 S + 0.8 E - 1.1 for independent standard normal scene and row effects. Draw the binary label with success probability sigmoid(1.8 z). Provider log odds are 1.3 z + 0.5 U + 0.5 V + 0.85 H + 0.5 J. U and V are normal scene-provider and row-provider terms shared by all checkpoints. H and J are stationary unit-variance Gaussian AR(1) processes over latent checkpoint order (correlation 0.65) at those same two levels. A single random permutation per provider per bank releases the checkpoints in the same order for A, B and I. The data generator has no change of domain. For a fixed frozen checkpoint vector the distribution on I is independent of its indices. Opportunity for selection is the intervention.

## Methods, endpoints and trajectory

* Primary method: ordinary native OKAPI with full-class Brier skill and the usual size objective. Start with eight provider singletons, 16 generations, eight survivors, population multiplier two, tournament size two, maximum 31 nodes and depth eight. The six stock operators and native fitness precision apply. Choose the final survivor with best unrounded fit Brier, breaking ties by smaller size and earlier final position.
* Secondary native search replaces predictive fitness and terminal choice by pooled positive-class F1 using the same threshold, structure, budget and tie rule. Record fit and audit Brier and F1 for the deployed tree, the best value among all visited programs, the best among final survivors and the trajectory by generation. Hindsight comparisons use I only after freezing the fitted choice. They are bounds relative to visited programs, with finite audit sampling noise.
* Secondary production reference: regularized native pattern procedure with the original 600-visit budget, variance penalty, its usual row-based 70/30 split and family refit. Its internal validation can share scenes with inner discovery; external I is scene-disjoint. This is a separate procedure from the two trajectory probes.
* Reference methods: arithmetic mean, full-class-Brier-ranked Top-N (fit selects the prefix), and standardized probability logistic stacking with C=1. Score all methods on fit and I, using full-class Brier and positive-class pooled F1. Record provider checkpoint choices, their average individual F1 on the selection block, opposite block and I, and selected family/prefix where relevant.

Primary contrast is audit Brier of Brier-trained native GP at K=8, reuse minus disjoint, averaged across the two swaps within each bank. Positive values indicate harm. Report a two-sided percentile 95% interval from 10000 paired bootstrap resamples of the 32 bank means, fixed bootstrap seed 97821. An interval above zero supports a loss of same-law transfer under this design; otherwise report uncertainty or a reversal according to interval direction. Also report the change in fit-to-audit optimism and the same audit contrasts for all controls, including F1 (larger is better) and the two other native methods. Other intervals are descriptive. The mean has no fitted fusion parameters and gives an exact zero audit contrast after swapping at all K. Compare audit minima across all visited and final programs to localize lost opportunities. Test audit-label invariance of the selected tree in an independent replay. No metric-based changes to the settings or additional banks after results.
