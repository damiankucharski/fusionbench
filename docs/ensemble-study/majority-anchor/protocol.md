# When the high-variance direction is a vote, a nuisance, or both

Round starts 2026-11-21 00:07:12 UTC. A single variance spike along one true majority direction allows it to be estimated with unlabeled covariance and oriented by the final labels. When that vote contradicts the known majority, the other two votes both equal the final label. Under independent standard Gaussian residuals, derive how their conditional mean and covariance recover the two remaining directions. Negative control: a variance spike entirely orthogonal to the true axes, where treating it as a vote is wrong. Additional mixed control: a spike at 45 degrees to one vote and a nuisance direction, where neither pure treatment is justified.

Freeze 16 independent seeds 1200–1215, d=9, 512 and 2048 nested training labels. Replace the absolute coordinate on the spike by an independent chi_7 variate, preserve its sign, recompute final majority labels on the resulting raw x. Include clean Gaussian data. Fit one rule using only raw training examples and final labels (no expert outputs): if largest/second eigenvalue ratio <2 use spherical angular estimator; otherwise orient the first principal component to positive agreement and treat it as a vote if agreement ≥0.625, else discard it before angular estimation. Compare automatic and all three forced routes, plus a 30-parameter dense smooth-majority network with random orthonormal initialization and zero biases, fit with 600 shared Adam steps of 64 samples at .015, deployed as median. Evaluate on 4096 fresh Gaussian queries and their paired sign-preserving relative-gain shift. Primary contrasts at n=2048 are automatic minus angular gain accuracy on true-vote spike, and automatic minus forced-anchor on nuisance spike. Report matched cosine, route frequencies, disagreement counts, the mixed case and all baselines. No claim of optimal thresholds or arbitrary mixed-direction correction.
