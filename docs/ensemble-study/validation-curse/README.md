# Is the validation winner the problem, or did search lose the useful trees?

Two rounds on the FusionBench and OKAPI research branches distinguish three effects: optimism from choosing among noisy validation scores, loss of useful programs during adaptive evolution, and distribution shift. Brier measures the sum of squared errors over all classes, averaged over examples. A smaller value is better. The standard panel uses the regularized native pattern procedure; the population probes separately instrument native GP to isolate the actual survivors.

## Round one: selection and candidate supply

* On 144 banks across all 24 standard scenarios, the selected discovery tree's apparent advantage over uniform averaging was 0.16914 Brier on internal validation and 0.16223 on an independent same-law audit. The estimated optimism of 0.00691 was positive (within-scenario 95% bootstrap interval 0.00258–0.01135). Reusing validation to choose a GP tree and then its family is part of this estimate. The same frozen trees are scored on both splits. [Panel report](panel/round1/README.md)
* With 64 fixed, analytically scored alternatives and 32 validation examples, a menu containing the optimal mean rejected it in all 32 null banks. With 512 validation examples it retained the mean in 24/32. This isolates selection among available candidates. [Fixed-menu audit](round1/README.md)
* On eight small redundant-pool null banks the optimum appeared in all active populations after one generation. It survived six generations in only three standard searches. The median survivor nonetheless improved. Keeping the cumulative archive available gave a different ceiling from restricting the oracle to the last generation. A genuinely informative candidate and nonlinear controls gave different trajectories. [Every generation, observer and fresh-test evaluation](trajectory/README.md)

## Round two: intervene in search with the same labels

For every candidate, compute the per-observation Brier improvement over the fixed arithmetic mean. Subtract one estimated standard error from the average improvement during GP selection, feature screening and native head fitting. Keep raw validation Brier for the final family decision. Search is derivative-free. The full-suite adapter keeps the incumbent's label budget, split, search cap, rounding and existing head regularization. Actual head and archive evaluation counts can vary between searches. A separate native option uses group means when the rows are dependent. [Prespecified confirmation](round2-lock.md) · [Native API](https://github.com/damiankucharski/OKAPI/blob/64ee67e/docs/api/core.md)

**The global penalty missed its primary target.** On 144 fresh banks the incumbent scored 0.21408 on the suite test, and penalized search scored 0.21606. The paired increase was 0.00198 (95% bootstrap interval +0.00042 to +0.00354, sampling banks within the 24 fixed scenarios). The corresponding mean, Top-N and probability LR scores were 0.37853, 0.33962 and 0.24545. Relative optimism fell by 0.00065 with an interval spanning zero. On small real classification banks, giving equal weight to digits, wine, iris and seeds, the change was +0.00004. These external partitions overlap and were studied previously. [Full-suite panel](panel/round2/README.md) · [Descriptive external check](real/README.md)

**A terminal holdout imposed an observable data cost.** With 448 instead of 640 rows, the inner incumbent lost 0.00586 on the source audit. Selecting among its frozen trees and the mean on the 192 outer rows recovered 0.00417. Requiring a multiplicity-adjusted improvement to leave the mean then cost 0.00203 relative to the unguarded outer decision. On the actual suite test the guarded outer strategy scored 0.21485 vs 0.21408 for the 640-row incumbent; the paired interval included zero. The retrospective test-best, an independent selection holdout, and adding the mean as an explicit fallback are distinct interventions. [Paired contrasts](panel/round2/summary.csv)

**When the optimal mean was present at initialization, it could still disappear.** With 24 training units and eight correlated annotations per unit, the ordinary native population lost a seeded true mean in all 12 null banks. The group-penalized search retained it in only one final population. A post-result simulation control replacing noisy training losses by their conditional expectations on the same training features retained the mean in all 12. On fresh runs without this seed the group penalty lowered observed test Brier from 0.76929 to 0.75544; mean, Top-N and LR scored 0.70687, 0.73022 and 1.08104 respectively. It also impaired learning in the positive-control setting. The mean's advantage over native GP and LR in this constructed null is consistent with a qualitative observation from the user's dissertation. The study did not measure MedMNIST or STARCOP performance. [Grouped experiment and control](grouped/README.md)

The new fitness and final selector are opt-in. A concrete next hypothesis is to preserve a small archive of fixed reference programs during evolution, testing separately whether this changes the final available population, its exploration trajectory and its deployable choice. The present witness establishes that removing a useful program can occur even when its rule is native and available initially. It does not establish that archive protection produces a better deployable rule.
