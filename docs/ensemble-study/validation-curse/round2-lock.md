# Second iteration: regularize evolution before the final candidates are chosen

The first standard-suite diagnostic measured 0.00691 relative selection optimism over 144 banks. On separate clustered multiclass banks the final retrospective best still exceeded the optimal mean by 0.04773 excess Brier, although it improved on initialization. With a single genuinely informative provider, the best available individual instead deteriorated from 0.00514 to 0.01261 despite improvement in median quality. Compare both mechanisms before recommending an algorithm change.

Primary intervention: subtract one standard error of paired loss improvement over arithmetic averaging from the training score. Apply it throughout candidate fitness, head fitting and discovery screening where applicable. Use all usual fitting rows and the same search allowance. Retain the incumbent raw held-out family-selection rule. Use standard-v3 with six fresh banks per scenario, seed roots 4620000/6620000 and the frozen round-one split sizes/streams. Report guard-minus-incumbent source-audit and deployment Brier, change in frozen-winner optimism and individual scenario reversals. Keep mean, Top-N and probability LR on identical budgets. Parameter z=1 is fixed; z=0 is the path-preservation control. Do not select hyperparameters from audit/test. Report whether the new rule improves held-out Brier and whether it reduces optimism as separate claims.

The real, active-survivor and clustered protocols in sibling folders specify additional endpoints. The clustered confirmation uses fresh independent banks and compares actual independent groups vs treating repeat annotations as independent. The outer holdout family switch is a secondary cost-of-data control. A retrospective candidate oracle gives diagnostic headroom; it is never a deployable method. No default promotion is planned from these two rounds.
