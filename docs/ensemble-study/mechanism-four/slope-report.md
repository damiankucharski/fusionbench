# Does the calibration gain depend on the simulator's provider response?

Round 5 keeps the previously confirmed policy fixed and changes a single component of the grouped rare-event simulator. In all three settings the latent event log-odds equal 1.8 times the underlying signal. We set the ordinary providers' logit slope to 1.3, 1.8 or 2.3. Changing slope changes the providers' reports, the trees discovered from them and the resulting calibration fitted on the source. At matched slopes the noisy provider scores need not equal calibrated event probabilities.

Each of twelve new data seeds supplies the same source and target examples, true event probabilities and residual draws for 24 combinations (three slopes times eight provider-error settings). Within a slope, the raw and calibrated variants start from precisely the same fitted predictions. Average the eight paired expected-Brier differences per seed before comparing slopes. The two-sided 95% interval resamples the twelve complete draws, 10,000 times, with seed 84105. [Frozen slope protocol](slope-protocol.md) · [Paired bank design](slope_banks.py)

**The expected Brier gain from calibrating the native GP largely disappeared at the matched slope.** The paired change in its calibration effect between provider slopes 1.3 and 1.8 was +.00169 (95% interval +.00101 to +.00235). This meets the registered criterion for a diminishing benefit. The corresponding change for calibrating arithmetic averaging was +.00280 (+.00187 to +.00364). This result identifies a dependence on how the generator scales providers' signals. The absolute level of risk also moves with the slope, which changes the information available in the reports.

All entries integrate Brier over both possible outcomes on the *same* audit inputs. The secondary difference between provider slopes 2.3 and 1.3 was +.00163 (+.00085 to +.00235) for the GP's calibration effect. At 1.3, fitted temperatures sharpened the GP in 51 of 96 bank conditions. At 1.8 only eight sharpened, and at 2.3 only two sharpened while four softened. Macro-F1 and the number of deployed providers stayed identical in each raw/calibrated factorial comparison. Binary average precision agreed to 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁶. **Across the rest of the benchmark**, the 30-family observed Brier fell from .34096 to .32377 under the same calibration (change −.01718, −.01908 to −.01517). The historical 27 changed from .36295 to .34444. Across the 30 family means 22 improved, two tied and six worsened. Examples include committee (−.10477) and cloned_shortcut (+.11340). The full-panel cheap references were mean .37542, ranked Top-N .35624 and probability stacking .26907; the split-based native-pattern reference scored .24310. On the earlier 16-scene task, calibrated averaging led at .13768 versus .14684 for calibrated GP, .15020 for raw GP and .17354 for logistic stacking. Weak specialization was a counterexample to broad calibration gains, raising GP Brier from .48485 to .48694.
