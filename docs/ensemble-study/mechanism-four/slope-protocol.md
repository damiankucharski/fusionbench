# Round 5. Change the simulator's response before interpreting temperature scaling

This is a new stress test, designed after the screening rounds with independent data. The round-4 candidate, validation criteria and results remain fixed. In our grouped rare-event simulator an ordinary provider uses slope 1.3 on the latent signal and the event log-odds have slope 1.8. A temperature gain could therefore depend on the simulator's chosen scale. The test below is specified before any round-5 model scores.

Run the same frozen cal_pull procedure and its uncalibrated pull_mean on the same data. Also use mean, equally calibrated mean, Top-N, probability LR, native full GP and the historical previous8. Train with complete fit labels, as before. Evaluate the eight registered heterogeneity × common-error × specialist cells at ordinary provider slopes 1.3, 1.8 and 2.3, with twelve *new* seeds starting at 496000. Within a seed all 24 worlds share the same fit/audit scenes, labels, true probabilities, residual draws and specialist mask. Only the specified provider mechanisms change. The central level matches the latent event-signal slope, while provider noise and other structure remain. This does not imply perfectly calibrated noisy provider forecasts at slope 1.8.

The primary statistic is the change in the effect of calibration at 1.8 versus 1.3: compute cal_pull minus pull_mean in conditional expected Brier, average the eight cells per seed and subtract the corresponding paired effect at 1.3. A positive difference with the lower 95% bound above zero supports a diminishing benefit at matched slopes. Report an unresolved or reversed result as such. Also report each calibration effect's own interval, 2.3 versus 1.3, and the same contrasts for calibrating the ordinary mean. Bootstrap all 12 draws as whole paired blocks, 10,000 times with seed 84105. Changes are diagnostic and do not replace round 4's confirmation decision.

Include the previous four synthetic controls (12 data seeds apiece, grouped controls from base+100, exact-law controls from base+200) and every one of the 30 default standard-v6 cases (three fresh seeds each from base+400). The 27 historical cases get their own panel. Report observed Brier, expected Brier where available, binary AP and macro-F1; per-case losses, deployment costs and calibration choices. There are 426 distinct bank conditions in this round. Keep the three slopes configurable as one public mechanism.
