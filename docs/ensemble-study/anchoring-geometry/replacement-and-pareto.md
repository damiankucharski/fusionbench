# Which trees should breed, and which tree should replace the incumbent?

The native solver joins parents and offspring, removes duplicate structures and retains a population. The first front includes predictive objectives and, when enabled, node count. If the front overflows, the solver ranks its members by Euclidean distance to the ideal predictive point. If it fits, the solver fills remaining slots from the dominated pool using the same distance. Parent tournaments also favor proximity on the predictive metrics. This gives a measured improvement a path to influence both present survival and future proposals. ## What the confidence rule means

For one child and one incumbent, compare both predictions on every independent validation unit. Average the reduction in loss. Hoeffding's inequality bounds how often this observed average can exceed its expectation by a stated margin when the unit contributions are independent and bounded. A simultaneous rule pays for all candidate comparisons it might select. Only a positive gain after the margin authorizes replacement. This rule can be used alongside an exploratory population in which unconfirmed children can reproduce. The eventual deployed program uses its own leaves.

In the two-context example the incumbent gives probability 0.5. A move to 0.9 or 0.1 saves 0.48 full-class Brier when correct and costs 1.12 when wrong. Its expected gain is positive above 70% agreement. With eight prespecified alternatives and a total error allowance of 0.05, the one-sided allowance is 0.00625 per alternative. Even allowing independent cases with different agreement probabilities averaging at most 0.7, the probability of 16 agreements in 16 cases is at most 0.7 raised to the sixteenth power, or 0.00332. The sharper Bernoulli bound can authorize that replacement. The Hoeffding margin exceeds the largest possible gain with 16 labels. At 64 active labels the minimum agreement counts are 45 for a positive observed gain, 56 for the Bernoulli bound and 58 for Hoeffding. These numbers are specific to this menu and these loss magnitudes. The error allowance is a repeated-sampling claim for a fixed design. It does not assign a probability to the chosen tree's future success. [Exact decision rule and enumeration](certificate/kl/README.md)

For a repeated adaptive search on the same labels, using 0.05 afresh per generation would invalidate this interpretation. One option is to give all encodable tree pairs weights in advance, including the coefficients they may fit. Another design would require data independent of the adaptive proposals. Clustered cases must be treated at the independent-unit level. The binary Brier calculation does not directly cover F1 or AP. [Assumptions for a fixed-design family of programs](README.md)

## Pareto dominance, default truncation and a check using STARCOP metrics

On a fixed full front, a unique winner on a primary score must survive dominance filtering if that score is one of the objectives. Additional objectives can affect which other trees survive, truncation to a finite population and later parents. They cannot change that unique winner when reselection uses precisely the same primary score on the same data and the whole front is retained. Different scoring precision, ties or a separate selection sample can change the conclusion.

The grouped experiment that lost the seeded optimal mean in all 12 banks had one predictive objective, training Brier, plus the deterministic size objective. Several noisy predictive criteria are unnecessary for that particular failure. MedMNISTv2 used AP and ROC-AUC, while STARCOP used F1, precision and recall, with optional size. The dissertation reports a validation-test F1 correlation of 0.23 across 520 STARCOP front occurrences. These trees share data and ancestors. This is descriptive evidence about ranking transfer. It does not identify the cause. [Grouped experiment](../validation-curse/grouped/README.md) · [Dissertation, sections 5.2 and 5.4](https://bip.polsl.pl/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2026/11/Damian-Kucharski_rozprawa-doktorska.pdf#page=112)

STARCOP computes F1 as the harmonic mean of precision and recall from one pooled confusion table. At exact precision, adding F1 to these two objectives preserves dominance, including when a common size objective is also present. Its addition still changes distances to the ideal. Separate rounding to three decimals can affect near ties. The following pair has ten positive and ten negative cases and equal program sizes. Order A then B.

| Tree | TP | FP | Precision | Recall | F1 | Squared distance on precision and recall | Squared distance including F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 7 | 1 | 0.8750 | 0.7000 | 0.7778 | 0.105625 | 0.155008 |
| B | 10 | 5 | 0.6667 | 1.0000 | 0.8000 | 0.111111 | 0.151111 |

Both vectors are nondominated. Calling the pinned native `paretoset` and `sort_by_optimal_point_proximity` on the two matrices retained both front members and reversed their proximity order from A–B to B–A, including after native three-decimal rounding. With one available slot, the survivor changes without adding information. No test outcomes were assigned to this example, so it makes no claim about the direction of generalization. ## Discriminating experiments

The first intervention worth testing is an incumbent and a separate breeding population. Include ordinary survival, strict admission to both roles and admission only for incumbent replacement with open breeding. Match total stored programs, actual candidate evaluations, mutation operators, data, seeds and the initial incumbent. Where possible replay proposals unchanged to isolate replacement, then use live runs to observe effects on lineage. An additional equal-budget control can store a simple incumbent with ordinary empirical replacement. Measure false displacement when the incumbent is optimal, power when a nonlinear descendant truly improves it, held-out scores and the number of providers needed for inference. Record which deployed trees descend from an initially unconfirmed program. The oracle, independent test and lineage observer never guide promotion. The default benchmark comparisons remain the full mean, ranked-prefix Top-N and logistic stacking.

An independent follow-up can swap the truncation policy while preserving the objectives and the deployment choice. NSGA-III assigns solutions on an overflowing front to reference directions and fills underrepresented niches. It targets coverage of different tradeoffs. It provides no statistical bound on label noise. The dissertation's NSGA-III comparison also changed representation to nonnegative fusion weights, so it cannot isolate the effect of this survivor policy inside OKAPI.
