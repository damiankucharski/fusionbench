# Recover the hidden majority axes from input directions under radial contamination

Round starts 2026-11-21 00:04:05 UTC. For a centered majority of three orthogonal signs, derive exact first and third angular moments when the input direction is uniform on the sphere. Positive radii may even depend on direction. Compare the earlier Gaussian estimator to normalized rows with the correct coefficient d/(d+1) and to normalized rows using the original unit coefficient. Check the rank of the corrected versus uncorrected population tensor without asserting their learned axes must differ. All methods see only raw inputs and final class labels.

Freeze d=9, fresh independent seeds 1100–1115, n=512, 2048, 8192, and 4096 independent Gaussian and within-row gain-shift queries per seed. Use the same base training observations in each paired condition. In radial contamination set 2% of training rows to forty times their original vectors. In the directional negative control replace projection along u1 by its original sign times a chi_7 variate, leaving other coordinates unchanged. This keeps all three signs and final labels. Its angular density is tilted by (u1·s)^6 and has a population sum-direction cosine of (3.2+2)/sqrt(3*(3.2²+2)) with the correct sum. A fixed common logit scale of five is used for all direct predictions.

Primary contrasts at n=2048: under radial contamination, angular versus Gaussian matched axis cosine and test gain-shift accuracy; on paired clean and radially rescaled training data, verify the predictions from angular training agree to ≤1e−10 for the identical test inputs. Report sample-size curves, limits under directional distortion, sum and axis cosines, Brier/accuracy, paired t intervals across seeds and exact population checks. No training-time optimizer or distilled targets.
