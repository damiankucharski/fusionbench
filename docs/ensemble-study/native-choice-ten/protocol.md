# When should a simpler fusion replace regularized native-pattern OKAPI?

Run exactly ten manual synthetic rounds. Every round includes all 30 standard-v6 families and four continuity controls (weak and strong stratified specialization, 16 and 128 training scenes). Compare the deployed regularized native-pattern OKAPI, arithmetic mean, full-training Brier-ranked best-prefix Top-N, and C=1 probability logistic stacking on each bank. The native reference uses GP seed 0, split seed 0, eight discovery-shortlisted GP finalists and the default regularized heads. The reference retains its original internal discovery/selection and final fit semantics. No external test labels, task family or target distribution inform a deployable choice. Full-class Brier is primary (smaller is better), macro-F1, provider count, switch frequencies and timing are secondary.

| Round | Question and prespecified rules |
|---|---|
| 1 | Does choosing the best apparent training Brier of mean, Top-N and the returned native estimator capture the benefit of simplicity? |
| 2 | Is the same choice on three out-of-fold source predictions per candidate more useful? Choose the lowest average held-out unit loss. |
| 3 | Does granting a simpler option one paired SE of tolerance relative to the OOF winner justify additional switches? Compare against round 2's criterion on the same banks. |
| 4 | Reverse the burden of evidence. Switch only if the best simple rule beats native by at least one paired SE. In a stricter arm require positive gain in all three outer folds as well. |
| 5 | Does requiring evidence across provider disagreement levels help? Add the requirement of nonnegative gain in both strata to the one-SE gain rule. Include the matched shortcut intervention below. |
| 6 | Add mean and a ranked-prefix tree as competitors on the native algorithm's existing validation rows. Train the prefix on discovery rows only and rerank on all rows if it wins. Keep the incumbent and its original tie priority. |
| 7 | Does the choice change with source size? Compare all six candidate rules on a randomly selected nested 128-row source and its 512-row parent, with fixed standard-suite audit rows. Keep any known scene intact. Continuity controls retain their original sizes. |
| 8 | How sensitive is the decision to the stochastic GP search? Compare all six under GP seeds 1 and 0 on identical standard banks, partitions and tests. Seed 1 is primary. |
| 9 | Select one of cv, one_se, paired, sign, stress and augmented by lowest average test Brier on new banks from the 24 previously declared development families, preferring that order for exact ties. Lock the choice and evidence before round 10. Keep the six omitted families and all controls out of selection. |
| 10 | Test the locked rule against native on independent draws. Primary success requires a negative upper 95% paired endpoint on all 30. For a wider claim also require an upper endpoint below +0.005 on the six withheld families and no more than +0.005 on weak specialization. Include the matched shortcut intervention. Descriptive results for other candidates cannot replace the selected rule. |

Use independent round blocks of data seeds starting at 720000 + 1000r, standard offset 400. Standard fits and tests use 512 rows except the R7 intervention. Use 3 seeds per standard family in R1–8, 5 in R9 and 10 in R10. For the four continuity controls use 8 seeds in R1–9 and 16 in R10, offsets 100 for scene banks and 200 for stratified banks. For the additional R5 and R10 shortcut control use offset 300, 8/16 independent source draws of 512 examples, region prevalence .05 in both splits and zero label and provider noise; evaluate exact risks as shortcut activity alone changes from 100% to 50% to 0%. All three target laws share exactly the same training data and the same 16 test prediction vectors, with different probabilities and masses. Score stationary controls and deliberate distribution shifts separately.

OOF choices use three group-disjoint folds for real scene IDs, row-stratified folds where feasible otherwise. Each row is held out once. Fit regularized native, mean and Top-N inside each fold; weight units equally (one scene or independent row). Preference order for ties is mean, Top-N, native. The one-SE simplicity arm considers the paired difference to the OOF winner. The paired-gain arm uses the standard error across units of native minus best simple Brier. It accepts zero gain when SE is also zero. The sign arm additionally requires strictly positive average gain in each outer fold. The stress arm instead requires nonnegative gain on both sides of the source median of unit-level mean squared provider disagreement (pool empty strata). These are training heuristics, without a nominal coverage guarantee after selection. Refit the chosen procedure on the full source. The internal validation of native itself still uses rows.

Use percentile paired bootstrap with 10,000 draws at 81000+r, resampling independent seeds within fixed standard families; never count rows within a scene as independent trials. R7/8 differences of treatment effects use the same data seed twice. Six withheld families are cloned_shortcut, parity, similar_classes_38, grouped_rare_event, shared_error_bloc, rare_scene_specialist. For R10 report how often a switch occurs, gains and losses conditional on switching, risk relative to all cheap comparators, and the retrospective headroom if mean/Top-N/native could be chosen with the test outcomes. The latter is descriptive and uses a finite test set.
