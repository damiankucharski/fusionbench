# Precision of native fitness selection, search regret and generalization

Frozen on 2026-11-21 before running this experiment. Standard native GP rounds the fitness used for selection to three decimal places, even though the winner archive can rank candidates by their unrounded Brier loss. The research option permits three, six or all available float64 decimals across all ordinary native scoring paths. A native fixture already shows that a Brier improvement smaller than a quantization step can be lost during one survivor decision. The present study asks how often selection is affected, how much empirical search regret changes, and whether any gain survives independently labeled examples. [Native implementation, parity and counterexamples](https://github.com/damiankucharski/OKAPI/blob/3eae0e5480cba0471cc5fda71b0c90f35b6a73f2/experiments/selection_precision/README.md)

## Interpretation and safeguards

Cawley and Talbot showed that fitting a model-selection criterion can lead to optimistic validation estimates; their Table 8 uses kernel classifiers and covers a different setting. Here the finite oracle and analytic population risk directly distinguish empirical optimization from population performance. Any observed effect is specific to this GP setup and repertoire. Confirm the fixed-budget native path against an independent stock replay; recover probabilities from serialized champions, verify the integer lattice minimum versus an independent enumeration, and reconcile costs. Retain the exact per-bank data needed to reconstruct the intervals.
