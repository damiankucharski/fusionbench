# Does a majority-of-three training prior help on genuine labels?

Started 2026-11-20 23:55:47 UTC. Fixed three tasks before fitting. Wisconsin diagnostic binary classes (all 569 sklearn rows) and sklearn handwritten-digit parity (all 1797 rows), using row-disjoint stratified fivefold cross-validation with seed 9921. The digit writer identities are unavailable. Third task is official UCI HAR walking upstairs versus downstairs, original activity IDs 2 vs 3. Train on the 21 official train subjects, evaluate once on the nine disjoint official test subjects, weighting each subject equally. Reuse the checked SHA-256 archive. Task labels in these datasets do not imply a known majority mechanism. No tuning or claims of three real biological/behavioral factors.

Fit standardization and nine-component whitened PCA on each training split only. In that common nine-dimensional representation compare the direct Gaussian first/third-moment estimate, smooth-training followed by median decoding initialized either by moment axes or random orthonormal axes, a three-hidden-unit ReLU single MLP, logistic regression C=1, and RBF SVC C=1 with internal training-only probability calibration. Add full standardized-input logistic/RBF and a full-width, 150-tree random forest, plus their equal probability average. Same 600 Adam minibatches of 64 and .015 learning rate as prior majority studies; seed fixed by task/fold. Primary outcome is Brier for moment-initialized network versus random-initialized network and versus equal probability ensemble, analyzed separately per dataset. Secondary accuracy. Report paired fold/subject values, aggregated scores, independent test units, training access, and the cost of the representation (PCA can be folded into each affine leaf). All experiments use the existing CPU only.
