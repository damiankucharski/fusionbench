# Which properties of a model pool can OKAPI recognize from training predictions?

In this experiment the disagreement between model predictions exposed shared model errors. Differences in the models' average training losses also revealed some differences in their underlying quality. Finding a specialist was harder. A statistic that rewards having the right model for each training example identified the presence of a specialist correctly in 32 of 64 cases, equal to chance.

We generated eight variations of the same training set for each of eight independent seeds. Every set had 128 examples from 16 scenes. Three switches independently changed the spread of the models' error scales, their share of common error, and the quality of a single model on a minority of scenes. The source fingerprint used only model probabilities and training labels, with scene IDs available for estimating uncertainty. Model identities were anonymous. The choice of statistic and expected direction for each switch was fixed before measuring its effects. [Protocol](protocol.md) · [Generator](banks_auto.py)

| Switch and its source statistic | Average change when switched on (95% interval) | Seeds in expected direction | Correct on held-out seeds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Unequal provider quality → larger standard deviation of provider Brier losses | +0.01203 [+0.00544, +0.01840] | 7/8 | 44/64 (68.8%) |
| Shared error → smaller squared distance from the mean prediction | −0.01301 [−0.01590, −0.01025] | 8/8 | 54/64 (84.4%) |
| Local specialist → larger gap between best fixed and best per-example provider | −0.000045 [−0.000352, +0.000256] | 5/8 | 32/64 (50.0%) |

The change column measures a controlled intervention. We varied one switch at a time, kept labels, model order and the other switches fixed, and averaged the four matched changes within each seed. Intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals from resampling those eight seeds. The last column answers a different question relevant to deployment. Given just **one** set of training predictions, can we recognize whether the switch was on? For each seed we used the other seven to choose a threshold for the designated statistic, then predicted all eight conditions of the held-out seed. Each switch was balanced, giving a 50% chance reference. The 64 conditions share eight independent draws. **Why did the specialist escape detection?** On average, 20% of scenes were eligible. There the simulator moved one provider's logit 85% of the way towards the true logit. Our statistic compares the lowest average Brier loss of any fixed provider with the average loss obtained by picking a provider after seeing each label. A model can win a row by chance. A model that better estimates the underlying probability can also lose that row after the outcome is sampled. Pooling across all rows hides where the improvement occurred. In the 32 matched comparisons the statistic increased 10 times, decreased 13 times and stayed equal nine times. This result leaves open whether scene-level repeated evidence or an observable cue could reveal the specialist. [Scenario implementation](../../../fusionbench/scenarios/grouped_rare_event.py)

For shared errors the interpretation is narrower than a universal correlation detector. Here, switching on common error increased mean residual cosine by 0.094 and reduced disagreement in every matched pair. On a different data-generating process, providers can also agree because they are all accurate, and residuals computed against the same noisy label inherit part of its variation. Selecting a fusion method requires measuring whether the choice improves held-out Brier. The subsequent rounds evaluate that separately.
