# Secondary comparison within series 1 and 3: spend the same slots during breeding

The terminal menus isolate proposal coverage. To test transfer during evolution, use the same 64 MIN/singleton fit and audit banks from series 1 and all 100 standard banks from series 3. Compare two live native searches on each bank. Both use the same OKAPI singleton founders and one all-provider mean, the same seeded tournaments, native crossover and mutation, fitness based on full-class Brier (higher-is-better skill rounded to three decimals for survival), and the native Pareto-then-proximity survivor rule including tree size. Cap depth at 8 and nodes at max(31,M+1). Start with min(M,8) singletons plus the mean. At each of 16 generations call propose_offspring(8) and score exactly eight new slots, including unchanged/duplicate programs. Reuse scored parents. Deduplicate structural keys for survival after scoring. Deploy the unrounded lowest-fit-Brier survivor, breaking ties by size and then survivor order. Both arms therefore spend 128 calls in addition to the same founders. This bounded native loop differs from the usual run_iteration loop in its accounting and proposal width.

In the intervention, replace the first ordinary offspring in odd-numbered generations by one of the eight unique probes scheduled as in the main experiment for the bank. This gives it the same chance to survive and reproduce as other candidates. The control keeps every offspring. The same search seed starts both arms, while different surviving populations can change later trajectories. Select without access to audit labels or planted probabilities. Record per-generation call counts, whether an inserted probe survives that generation, and retained and deployed genotype. Compare paired independent audit excess on the MIN and singleton laws and observed Brier on all 25 fixed families. Use bank-level percentile bootstrap, 10,000 draws, seed 320141; resample banks within fixed families for the panel. These are secondary contrasts; report over all banks regardless of the primary results. Both cohorts share benchmark controls with their respective primary experiment.
